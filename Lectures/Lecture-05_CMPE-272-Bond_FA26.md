## Slide 11: What is SDLC?

- A systems development life cycle is composed of a number of clearly defined and distinct work phases
- Contract between Stakeholders and IT on the process that will be followed for developing solutions

---

## Slide 12: Why do we need SDLC?

- To bring consistency across project teams in terms of process – common process ‘Language’
- To make solution development process repeatable
- To ensure teams understand deliverables from each stage.
- To ensure teams are following same templates for deliverables

---

## Slide 13: How will it change what we do today?

- We still implicitly follow a process. Following a formal SDLC will make process steps explicit, clear and consistent
- Will incorporate ROI, checks and balances for projects. If the cost justification isn’t there, then project will be canceled
- Some of the tasks that we are doing in later stages will be required to be done in the initial stages. This will help identifying issues early in the life cycle.
- Will have more formal planning & tracking for major milestones like ‘Readiness Review’ rather than leaving it for the last minute
- Will have some impact on our timelines but –
- Will help us in developing right solutions first time itself
- Will help in more efficient project planning and management

- The idea is not to make process very heavy and documentation centric – but to document information consistently in right documents (and not in emails and people’s head).

---

## Slide 14: What SDLC covers/Does NOT cover?

- Covers life cycle for an enhancement request/New Application once the preassessment (Business Commit) for the request is completed
- Does NOT cover Enhancement Requests Consolidation and Prioritization Process
- Does NOT cover process for small projects (a.k.a. operational items)

## Slide 26: Scrum Basics

- Scrum is an agile process that allows us to focus on delivering the highest business value in the shortest time.

- The business sets the priorities. Teams self-organize to determine the best way to deliver the highest priority features.

- Projects progress via a series of sprints. In keeping with an agile methodology, sprints are timeboxed to no more than a month long, most commonly two weeks

- A planning meeting starts the sprint – team members figure out how many items they can commit to, and then create a sprint backlog – a list of the tasks to perform during the sprint
- During an agile Scrum sprint, the Scrum team takes a small set of features from idea to coded and tested functionality. At the end, these features are done, meaning coded, tested and integrated into the evolving product or system
- On each day of the sprint, all team members should attend a daily Scrum meeting, including the ScrumMaster and the product owner. This meeting is timeboxed to no more than 15 minutes.

## Slide 31: Kanban for Software Development

- 

- 

- 

- 

David J Anderson created “K”anban, now known as Kanban Method. Kanban for knowledge work is a management method of just-in-time delivery based on capacity to do work. As such, here the focus is not to overload the team members.
In Kanban, the process workflow, from definition of work to its delivery to the customer, is displayed for participants to see and team members pull work from each queue.
Kanban for software development means a visual process management system that tells what to produce, when to produce it, and how much to produce- inspired by the Toyota Production System and Lean Manufacturing.
For more details on “Kanban (Method)”, please check out David J. Anderson’s website: http://www.djaa.com/principles-kanban-method-0 and his excellent book:
Kanban: Successful Evolutionary Change for Your Technology Business by David J. Anderson, Publisher: Blue Hole Press (April 7, 2010)

## Slide 75: GitHub Projects

- GitHub Projects is GitHub’s project management and planning tool integrated

directly with repositories.
- It provides kanban-style boards, timelines, and automation features for

tracking issues, pull requests, and tasks.
- Used widely in agile, DevOps, and enterprise workflows to connect code with

project planning.
- Single Source of Truth: Developers, PMs, and stakeholders can see tasks,

progress, and code in one place.
- Bridges Development & Management: Links planning (roadmaps, priorities)

directly to execution (commits, issues, PRs).
- Supports Modern Methodologies: Fits agile (sprints, backlogs), kanban

(continuous flow), and hybrid enterprise methods.

---

## Slide 76: Projects: Core Features

- Boards: Kanban-style columns (To Do, In Progress, Done).
- Timelines: Gantt-like views for roadmap planning.
- Custom Fields: Add priority, status, points, assignee, etc.
- Issue & PR Integration: Automatically sync work items with repository activity.
- Automation: Rules to auto-move cards when issues close, PRs merge, or labels

change.
- Insights: Charts for burndown, velocity, and bottleneck tracking.
